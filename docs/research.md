# Research and optimization decisions

Sources inspected on 2026-09-20:

- [Original MOSS model card](https://huggingface.co/OpenMOSS-Team/MOSS-Audio-Tokenizer): identifies the requested 24 kHz checkpoint and streaming API constraints. Do not substitute the newer v2 model. Local pinned source is authoritative for exact arithmetic and cache behavior.
- [MOSS paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.10934): architecture/training background; its claims do not establish speedups for this implementation.
- [PyTorch CUDA graphs article](https://pytorch.org/blog/accelerating-pytorch-with-cuda-graphs/) and [CUDA semantics documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/main/notes/cuda.html): motivate eliminating repeated host launch overhead while retaining stable graph tensor addresses. Capture setup is measured separately; output clones prevent replay aliasing.
- [Triton layer normalization tutorial](https://triton-lang.org/main/getting-started/tutorials/05-layer-norm.html): reduction and fusion reference. A new reduction order can change codec decisions; normalization fusion is not yet promoted.
- [CuTe DSL documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/cutlass/4.3.2/media/docs/pythonDSL/cute_dsl.html) and [NVIDIA elementwise tutorial](https://github.com/NVIDIA/cutlass/blob/main/examples/python/CuTeDSL/cute/ampere/tutorial/elementwise_add_autotune.py): launch/framework integration reference. Implementation uses installed CuTe 4.7.1 API. SM100-only tensor instructions are inappropriate for this GeForce SM120 GPU.

The residual kernel uses two rounded FP32 operations, rather than FMA, to match upstream multiply then add. Triton sets `enable_fp_fusion=False`; CuTe emits `mul.rn.f32` followed by `add.rn.f32`. Cancellation tests exercise this difference.

Initial reduced-model profiling identifies 66 weight-normalization calls per encode, accounting for 18.3% of recorded device operator time in that fixture. Its quantizer dimensions/depth are retained, but transformer depth/width are reduced: percentages must not be extrapolated to the full checkpoint. Frozen weight materialization and codebook normalization are now cached without changing operations or parameter tensors.

Future investigations: repeated RoPE tables/masks across transformer layers; fused ring-cache updates; strict-FP32 quantizer nearest-neighbor kernels preserving near-tie decisions; the many small 1x1 quantizer convolutions; GEMM shapes and bandwidth limits of 6+ GB FP32 parameters. Measure each change in isolation, reject fidelity failures, and retain regressions in the evidence log.

Confirmed streaming defect: upstream `RingKVCache.complete` writes a whole chunk before attention into a ring of `context` entries. With multiple local tokens per chunk, this overwrites history needed by early queries once the ring fills. A uniform-attention regression demonstrates an incorrect average of 2.5 instead of 2.0. Sessions now allocate `context + chunk_tokens - 1` entries at each transformer resolution, retaining the unchanged context mask. Tests verify both the analytic counterexample and capacity throughout encoder/decoder stages. Full-checkpoint long-duration comparisons against offline causal attention remain required; matching corrected eager streaming alone is insufficient.

## Full-checkpoint findings and new kernels

The completed 80 ms full-checkpoint profile records 306 encode matrix multiplications (5.30 ms, 49.1% of device operator time) and 273 decode matrix multiplications (5.26 ms, 55.8%). Unprofiled wall times are 46.15/45.17 ms, showing substantial host-launch gaps. Quantizer weight normalization is only 1.4% of full encode device time; the earlier 18.3% fixture percentage was not representative of the whole model.

The RoPE implementation emitted roughly a thousand elementwise multiplications per encode. A new Triton kernel rotates Q and K together, using PyTorch's original sin/cos results and separate FP32 arithmetic to retain bitwise equality. Static frequencies are cached. Full-model ablation on 240 ms inputs reduces graph encode from approximately 11.8 to 10.5 ms and decode from 10.0 to 8.7 ms. Exact corpus checks pass with both Triton and CuTe residual kernels.

The new two-launch Triton KV path preserves active-lane values/positions, skips writes for paused lanes, and advances offsets on device. Amortized cache-update measurements improve approximately 2.7–14.7× depending on batch/cache shape; these are component timings. Full two-lane pause/resume/reset tests match independent timelines exactly. A 12.8-second full-model music test has exact streaming/offline token equality. Decoder streaming/offline differences (maximum 1.70e-6, RMSE 4.99e-8) already exist in corrected eager execution; the optimized stream is bitwise identical to that eager reference. Broader contexts/content still need validation.

Additional primary sources: [Triton fused attention tutorial](https://triton-lang.org/main/getting-started/tutorials/06-fused-attention.html), [NVIDIA PTX floating-point semantics](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/parallel-thread-execution/), and [NVIDIA RTX 5070 Ti bandwidth specification](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/studio-ai-geforce-rtx-5070-ti-gpu-dlss/). A new attention reduction would need new numerical evidence; the fused-attention example's lower-precision performance is not an FP32 result.

`results/weight_storage.json` records a checkpoint storage inspection. The sampled matrices are not exactly representable in FP16/BF16, so simply casting weights would violate bitwise preservation. At the published 896 GB/s peak bandwidth, reading all dense FP32 transformer matrices once per direction alone takes about four milliseconds. This is an inference under the dense, per-call weight-read assumption, not a universal bound on every future algorithm or hardware choice. It explains why a 100× claim against a roughly 40–50 ms GPU baseline is unsupported by current evidence. Continue with matrix-kernel profiling, reduction-preserving fusion, table/mask sharing, and measured batching rather than changing the baseline to manufacture a speedup.


## Shared tables, matrix experiments, and batching

Stage-shared RoPE tables now retain exact outputs in full-model offline, long-stream, and independently paused/reset lane checks. Sharing requires known synchronized offsets; external streaming with divergent offsets falls back. The 240 ms batch-one graph improves to 10.158 ms encode / 8.391 ms decode. Causal-mask sharing remains a separate candidate.

Actual checkpoint matrix experiments use the [Triton matrix multiplication tutorial](https://triton-lang.org/main/getting-started/tutorials/03-matrix-multiplication.html), [tl.dot precision documentation](https://triton-lang.org/main/python-api/generated/triton.language.dot.html), and [CUTLASS functionality table](https://docs.nvidia.com/cutlass/latest/media/docs/cpp/functionality.html) as implementation references. `input_precision="ieee"` avoids TF32 input truncation but does not force the vendor reduction order. Naive tiled variants generally regress. Specialized row reductions sometimes improve warm-cache GEMV substantially; gains fall to roughly 1.1–1.3× with cache eviction. Their full-model experiment offers only about 1.7% combined latency improvement and changes waveform rounding despite exact tokens on 11 short inputs. These paths remain experimental, with numerical differences recorded rather than inferred to be perceptual quality loss.

NVIDIA's [floating-point emulation article](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/unlocking-tensor-core-performance-with-floating-point-emulation-in-cublas/) describes BF16x9 FP32 emulation using multiple components/cross-products. The [cuBLAS documentation](https://docs.nvidia.com/cuda/cublas/) support table inspected on 2026-09-20 restricts this floating-point FP32 route to compute capabilities 10.0/10.3 with CUDA 12.9 or later. This RTX 5070 Ti is SM12.0 and is not listed. Fixed-point FP64 emulation support on 12.x is a different feature. Do not assume upgrading CUDA provides BF16x9 here; no such path or reduced-precision weights have been enabled.

Matched batching demonstrates a distinct throughput/latency tradeoff: batch 128 reaches 353.5/364.1 audio seconds per second encode/decode while only improving 1.06×/1.08× over equal-batch upstream eager. Its profile is roughly 80% SIMT SGEMM and 10% efficient attention. Thus matrix arithmetic dominates at larger batches, while graph launch elimination explains much of the small-batch gain. Both measurements guide further work; neither supports a 100× matched-workload claim.


## Fused and shared attention masks

The pinned upstream attention builds per-layer boolean masks from 64-bit query/key positions. A Triton kernel now computes the same causal/context tests and can write the FP32 additive bias directly, with row strides aligned to eight elements. `attention_mask_backend="triton"` enables it; synchronized stage calls reuse the bias while retaining separate KV tensors. External upstream streaming uses per-layer masks. Query/key/value projection, rotary arithmetic, SDPA, and output projection remain unchanged.

The [PyTorch 2.8.0 attention implementation](https://github.com/pytorch/pytorch/blob/v2.8.0/aten/src/ATen/native/transformers/attention.cpp) confirms that its ordinary boolean-mask conversion uses zero and negative infinity, followed by eight-element alignment for memory-efficient attention. Directly constructing these values avoids conversion and padding on every layer. This applies to the tested CUDA FP32 route; cuDNN's separate boolean conversion uses a finite sentinel, so do not generalize this implementation to reduced-precision/cuDNN paths. [SDPA documentation](https://docs.pytorch.org/docs/main/generated/torch.nn.functional.scaled_dot_product_attention.html) describes the boolean/additive semantics.

A shared-boolean intermediate reached 9.719/7.945 ms graph encode/decode; direct aligned additive construction reached 9.491/7.821 ms. The matched full upstream eager baseline for the final run was 48.369/38.571 ms, giving 5.10×/4.93×. Both residual backends retain exact outputs in the 12-case corpus, and corrected long streaming remains exact. Changes to attention reductions have not been introduced.

The new filled-ring profile (batch two, 80 ms chunks, full ten-second history) measures 11.665 ms encode and 10.208 ms decode per push. Dense GEMM kernel groups take roughly half the device time; memory-efficient attention takes 26.6% encode / 29.7% decode. Therefore streaming attention is now a substantial optimization target alongside weight-bandwidth-limited small-M matrices. Repeated cache offset operations remain candidates, but their individual kernels are below 1% each in this profile.


## FP32 split-cache attention research

Actual filled-ring activations confirm four attention geometries per direction at batch two / 80 ms chunks: (heads, queries, keys, width) = (20,1,125,64), (12,2,251,64), (12,4,503,64), (12,8,1007,64). The first geometry occurs in 32 layers; each other geometry occurs in 12. `benchmarks.attention_shapes` captures representative inputs after the ring fills, preserving their strides, and compares vendor SDPA, its math backend, an explicit FP32 Triton split-cache reduction, and optional compiled FlexAttention. Warm-cache and cache-evicted component times are separated from model measurements.

The [Flash-Decoding article](https://pytorch.org/blog/flash-decoding/) motivates splitting the key/value sequence to increase parallel work when query length is short. Its published lower-precision long-context speedups are not this codec's FP32 performance. Our prototype computes per-split maxima, denominators, and weighted-value sums, followed by a stable merge. Fully masked rows return zero. No TF32, FP16/BF16 operands, FP8/FP4, or weight conversion is used. Reduction order and exponential rounding differ from vendor SDPA, so this is an experimental numerical alternative, not an exact replacement.

`results/attention_shapes.json` records roughly 3–7× warm component improvements for the 64-key split, with smaller gains after cache eviction. Against FP64 diagnostics, error is often smaller than vendor FP32 but not uniformly so; closer-to-FP64 component results do not prove codec quality. The [FlexAttention inference article](https://pytorch.org/blog/flexattention-for-inference/) supplied a second comparison. Installed PyTorch 2.8 uses a quoted source literal for its precision option (`"'ieee'"`); the experiment explicitly requests it and disables TF32. FlexAttention is faster than SDPA here but slower than the custom split prototype, and is also nonbitwise (`results/attention_flex.json`).

Full streaming measurements use music, speech, and environmental audio at 12.8 seconds per lane; recordings shorter than that are explicitly repeated, with repetition counts in the report. Across two lanes and all 32 quantizers, all 30,720 tokens match the existing optimized stream. With fixed reference codes, maximum waveform differences are 1.69e-6 music, 2.10e-5 speech, and 8.51e-5 environmental audio, with respective signal-to-error ratios of 126.4, 118.2, and 102.0 dB. This is numerical evidence, not a listening study or universal no-quality-loss result. The prototype stays outside the supported runtime.

The prototype improves encoder latency from about 11.6–11.8 to 9.35–9.37 ms and decoder from about 10.13 to 7.84–7.85 ms per filled-ring push (1.24–1.26× / about 1.29× against the already optimized graph). These are approximately 20–21% / 22–23% latency reductions, not another 5× whole-model result. Further work should tune query reuse, examine near-tie quantizer decisions on more diverse recordings, and investigate attention reduction order if bitwise replacement is required.

A targeted test exposed stride-dependent Triton reduction trees when graph cloning densifies gapped Q/K/V tensors. The experimental wrapper now canonicalizes Q/K/V layout before launch and prevents specialization on bias row strides, which also affected the 256-key variant. Captured model buffers are already contiguous and require no copies; gapped caller inputs pay explicitly for normalization. Eager/graph equality is verified separately from tolerance-based comparison with vendor SDPA.
